# HUTTY 3.1 — PHASE 01
## Complete Product Audit, Calculator Mapping & Implementation Blueprint

### ROLE

Act as a senior product architect, construction-estimation systems analyst, full-stack engineer and product UX researcher.

You are working on Hutty, a guided home-planning and construction-estimation platform.

Your task is to thoroughly inspect the existing application and produce an implementation blueprint based on the attached document:

**Hutty_Product_Vision_and_Developer_Brief.docx**

This document is the primary source of product requirements. Read all 10 pages, line by line. Do not skip sections, tables, implementation notes, restrictions or acceptance criteria.

Do not start implementing the redesign yet.

---

## 1. Primary objective

We need to understand:

1. What Hutty currently does.
2. What functionality already exists.
3. How the existing calculator works.
4. Which inputs feed which calculations.
5. How quantities, rates and BOQ items are connected.
6. What the client wants changed.
7. What functionality is missing.
8. What can be redesigned without backend changes.
9. What requires additional engineering.
10. What must be protected from accidental modification.

Do not assume the previous development reports accurately describe the current code. Verify everything against the actual repository.

## 2. Inspect the complete application

Inspect the following areas wherever they exist:

- Public homepage and marketing pages.
- Calculator entry and all existing calculator stages.
- Plot and building configuration.
- Room and space inputs.
- Construction material selections.
- Flooring and tile selections.
- Doors and windows.
- Electrical and plumbing.
- Painting and fabrication.
- Estimate generation.
- Detailed BOQ.
- PDF generation.
- Login and registration.
- Anonymous or guest access.
- Customer dashboard.
- Projects and saved estimates.
- Payment history.
- Cashfree checkout and verification.
- Consultation booking.
- Admin dashboard and every Admin group.
- Material rates and pricing configuration.
- Formula management.
- Analytics and tracking.

Inspect the actual source files, routes, API calls, database models, configuration and calculation dependencies.

Where possible, run the existing application and inspect the actual screens.

Do not claim that something works merely because a component or API exists.

## 3. Build a complete calculator input inventory

Create a structured inventory of every existing calculator input.

For every input, document:

- Existing screen or chapter.
- Field name.
- What information it collects.
- Data type and validation.
- Whether it is mandatory or optional.
- Default value.
- Where the value is stored.
- Which calculation uses it.
- Which downstream values depend on it.
- Whether the customer should enter it or Hutty should calculate it.
- Whether the client wants it retained, moved, improved or added.

Do not remove or simplify existing fields just because they appear technical.

## 4. Map the complete calculation and BOQ flow

This is one of the most important tasks.

For every relevant construction category, create a mapping:

**Customer input → Formula/dependency → Calculated quantity → Customer selection → Rate → Cost → BOQ item → Report output**

Cover at least:

- Plot and site details.
- Setbacks and preliminary buildable area.
- Floors and building configuration.
- Rooms and floor-wise areas.
- Concrete and reinforcement steel.
- Cement, sand and aggregates.
- AAC blocks, clay bricks and concrete blocks.
- Flooring, tiles, skirting and dado.
- Doors and windows.
- Electrical points, wiring and switches.
- Plumbing, sanitaryware and fittings.
- Paint and surface areas.
- Railings, gates and fabrication.
- Elevation and cladding.
- Labour and installation.
- Taxes and contingency, where supported.

For each calculation, identify:

- Actual formula or calculation function.
- Input dependencies.
- Units.
- Assumptions.
- Rounding and precision behaviour.
- Existing validation.
- Whether the quantity is calculated, estimated or manually entered.
- Whether the resulting amount reconciles with quantity × rate.
- Whether the result appears in the BOQ and PDF.

If a mapping cannot be verified, mark it as UNKNOWN. Do not invent formulas or assume functionality.

## 5. Compare the existing calculator against the client's seven chapters

Map the existing application into the following required journey:

### Chapter 01 — Tell us about your plot
Location, plot dimensions, planning authority, road width, orientation, setbacks and preliminary buildable area.

### Chapter 02 — Tell us about your dream home
Floors, parking, building use, rooms, floor-wise configuration, built-up area and feasibility comparison.

### Chapter 03 — Choose your core construction materials
Steel, cement, concrete grade, walling material and related quantities.

### Chapter 04 — Choose flooring and tiles for each space
Living/dining, master bedroom, other bedrooms, bathrooms, kitchen, balcony, utility, staircase and other spaces.

Must support individual choices, apply-to-similar-spaces and individual overrides.

### Chapter 05 — Choose doors and windows
Main entrance, internal doors, bathroom doors, sliding doors, windows, frames, glazing and hardware.

Must remain a dedicated chapter.

### Chapter 06 — Select electrical, plumbing and other finishes
Electrical, plumbing, painting, fabrication, elevation and cladding.

### Chapter 07 — Review your estimate and BOQ
Preliminary cost, cost per square foot, work-wise breakdown, material quantities, specifications, rates, assumptions and paid detailed-report offer.

For each chapter classify every requirement as:

- IMPLEMENTED AND VERIFIED.
- PARTIALLY IMPLEMENTED.
- NOT IMPLEMENTED.
- IMPLEMENTED BUT NEEDS IMPROVEMENT.
- UNKNOWN — REQUIRES INVESTIGATION.

Provide evidence from the actual code or working application.

## 6. Audit the customer journey

Trace the actual user journey from homepage to report delivery.

Investigate:

- Whether a visitor can enter the calculator without registration.
- Whether anonymous sessions exist.
- How progress is stored.
- Whether progress survives refresh.
- Whether save/resume works.
- How mobile/email is collected.
- When registration becomes mandatory.
- When the free estimate appears.
- How the paid report is presented.
- How checkout is initiated.
- How Cashfree payment is verified.
- How the report is generated.
- How the report is delivered and retrieved.

Identify every friction point and missing requirement.

Do not change the existing authentication or payment implementation during this audit.

## 7. Audit estimate, BOQ and PDF integrity

Check whether:

- Quantities reconcile with displayed rates and amounts.
- Units are consistent.
- Rounding is consistent.
- Dependent quantities update when upstream inputs change.
- Material and labour costs are distinguishable.
- Taxes and contingency are properly identified.
- Assumptions and exclusions are visible.
- The free estimate and paid report offer different levels of value.
- PDF layout is readable and professionally formatted.
- The generated report contains the correct customer's project information.

Do not modify calculation or PDF logic.

Document defects with reproduction steps and evidence.

## 8. Audit the existing Admin and rate system

Inspect all Admin functionality, including:

- Material rate management.
- Regional pricing.
- Rate sources and update dates.
- Formula configuration.
- Draft, publish and rollback workflows.
- User management.
- Leads and consultations.
- Project management.
- Audit logs and system settings.

Determine how material selections from the customer calculator connect to Admin-managed rates and the production calculation engine.

Identify any missing mapping or broken connections.

## 9. Audit analytics

Check whether the following events are already implemented:

- landing_view
- calculator_start
- chapter_started
- chapter_completed
- estimate_generated
- boq_preview_viewed
- checkout_started
- payment_success
- payment_failed
- report_downloaded

Document existing tracking, event payloads, missing events and whether the data can identify funnel drop-offs by chapter, device and campaign.

Do not invent analytics data.

## 10. Review the visual and language experience

This is a product audit, not a redesign.

Inspect the current interface and identify:

- Technical language that ordinary homeowners may not understand.
- Long or confusing forms.
- Missing visual material selections.
- Missing quantity explanations.
- Repetitive layouts.
- Generic or unsuitable imagery.
- Poor information hierarchy.
- Mobile usability problems.
- Confusing payment and report screens.
- Unclear error, loading and empty states.

Provide actual examples of existing text and proposed simpler alternatives.

Record where original illustrations, diagrams, floor plans or custom graphics would materially improve understanding.

Do not implement the new visual identity yet.

## 11. Protected systems and restrictions

Do not make production changes during this phase.

In particular, do not modify:

- Calculation engine.
- Existing formulas.
- Backend APIs.
- Database schema or migrations.
- Customer or project records.
- Cashfree service or payment verification.
- Authentication and authorisation.
- PDF calculation or generation.
- Admin functionality.
- Production configuration.
- Environment variables or secrets.

Do not delete or replace existing features.

Do not simplify the calculator into a square-foot rate calculator.

Do not remove dedicated flooring or doors/windows selections.

Do not introduce mandatory registration before calculator entry.

Do not commit, push or deploy.

If you discover a critical bug, document it rather than fixing it in this phase.
