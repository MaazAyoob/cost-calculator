Hutty Calculator
Developer UX/UI Brief · Mobile-first home construction planning experience
Design principle: Simple for the homeowner. Intelligent behind the scenes. The calculator should feel like
planning a future home—not filling out a technical construction form.
Visual design reference
The illustration is a visual direction reference. Example costs and report prices in the mockup are placeholders, not approved Hutty pricing or
calculated estimates.
1. Brand and visual direction
• Primary green: #1B3D34; accent orange: #F28C28.
• Use warm-white backgrounds, rounded cards, subtle borders and readable sans-serif typography.
• Use visual choices for floors, rooms, construction types and finishes instead of technical dropdowns wherever possible.
• Avoid crowded screens, excessive text, distracting animations and complex technical forms.
2. Eight-stage calculator flow
Stage
What to collect / show
1 · Your Plot
City/location, plot dimensions or area, land status and planning authority. Include “Not sure”.
2 · Your Home
Floor count, purpose (family/rental/farmhouse), parking and intended start timeline.
3 · Your Rooms
Bedrooms, bathrooms, living/dining, kitchen/utility and optional spaces such as pooja room,
balcony, study, store or terrace.
4 · Your Buildable Space
Setback knowledge, sanctioned plan/rough sketch, site conditions and demolition. Derive
preliminary footprint and built-up area from relevant planning inputs.
5 · Construction Style
Structural system or “Not sure”, walling type, quality level and whether structural drawings are
available.

3. Interaction requirements
• One primary question or decision per screen; show progress with meaningful stage names.
• Update a simple house illustration when major design choices change.
• Use conditional logic to avoid irrelevant questions and preserve answers when users go back.
• Use subtle transitions around 180–300 ms; support reduced-motion preferences and keyboard navigation.
• Make “Not sure” valid wherever possible; show clear errors and prevent accidental progress loss.
• Do not require account creation before a useful preliminary estimate. Ask for sign-in when saving, purchasing or retrieving a
report.
4. Calculation and trust requirements
• Keep the UI separate from the calculation engine. The UI collects homeowner-friendly inputs; the engine derives quantities,
applies rates and records assumptions.
• Never hardcode illustrative prices or quantities in production. Show actual outputs from the calculation engine.
• Unknown planning or structural information must be visible as an assumption and reduce estimate certainty. Do not imply an
estimate is a sanctioned design or engineer-approved structural calculation.
• Separate material costs from labour. Show applicable GST transparently and state inclusions, exclusions and rate validity.
5. Analytics and conversion funnel
• Track landing visits, calculator starts, per-stage completion/abandonment, preliminary estimate views, detailed report previews,
sign-ups, checkout starts, successful payments and report downloads.
• Optimize first for successful completion of the preliminary estimate, then conversion to the detailed BOQ.
6. Implementation order
• Phase 1: Landing page, guest-first calculator and progress flow.
• Phase 2: Calculation summary, transparent cost breakdown and editable selections.
• Phase 3: Detailed BOQ preview, account creation, payment and report delivery.
• Do not delay the core calculator redesign for advanced marketplace features.
Acceptance criteria
A first-time homeowner can enter plot details, configure a basic home and reach a preliminary estimate
without creating an account or understanding technical construction terminology. The experience is
responsive, reliable and connected to the real calculation engine.
